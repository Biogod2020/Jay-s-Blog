## Research Philosophy & Clinical Background
I am an M.D. candidate in the 8-year program at Fudan University. My background spans clinical medicine and computational biology, driven by the challenge of decoding the spatiotemporal evolution of neurodegenerative and neuropsychiatric diseases. Traditional clinical pathways often struggle with the complexity of these disorders, and standard bioinformatics pipelines are increasingly outpaced by the sheer volume of multi-modal biological data. 

I view the integration of artificial intelligence—specifically multi-modal foundation models and multi-agent systems—as an essential paradigm shift in scientific discovery. My core motivation is to bridge computational methodologies with clinical truths: to effectively treat patients at the bedside, we must first structure and interpret the fragmented data in the computational realm.

## Spatial Omics & Agentic Curation
A significant bottleneck in computational biology is the accessibility of high-quality data. To address the challenge of unstructured "dark data" in spatial omics, I developed **SpatialDataAgent**, an autonomous AI framework designed to curate paired Histology-Spatial Transcriptomics (H&E-ST) data. Utilizing an iterative ReAct loop, an *Evolutionary Standardizer*, and an *Objective Scorer*, the framework dynamically processes geometric anomalies and aligns spatial coordinates. Deployed across public repositories, this system autonomously recovered 769 H&E-ST paired datasets—yielding a 141% improvement over manual curation methods—and generated **HESRT**, a standardized datalake comprising 29.2 million spots and 29.9 gigapixels of imaging data.

Beyond data infrastructure, I apply spatial intelligence to uncover biological mechanisms. By integrating *in situ* β-amyloid staining with single-nucleus sequencing, my colleagues and I constructed a 12.36 million-profile spatiotemporal atlas of Alzheimer's disease. Through computational algorithms quantifying spatially tuned transcriptional modules, we identified the SUPAR microglia population and its mechanistic link to basal forebrain cholinergic neuron vulnerability.

## Future Focus: Multimodal Models & Diagnostic Agents
My current and future research focuses on advancing AI systems for clinical neuroscience through two primary tracks:

1. **Efficient Multi-modal Scientific Discovery**: Leveraging large-scale datasets like HESRT, I aim to train multi-modal foundation models that align high-resolution histopathology images with spatial transcriptomic signatures. Rather than relying solely on parameter scaling, my focus is on developing efficient, elegant training methodologies (e.g., advanced contrastive learning and GNN-based alignments) to extract generalizable biological rules and predict neurodegenerative states directly from unannotated tissue morphology.

2. **Multi-Agent Clinical Reasoning**: To address high misdiagnosis rates in complex neuropsychiatric and rare neurological disorders, I am developing multi-agent diagnostic frameworks. By simulating human clinical consortiums using collaborative agent topologies and "enumeration-then-elimination" reasoning strategies, these systems are designed to systematically analyze complex patient histories, differential diagnoses, and multi-modal data to provide accurate, highly interpretable diagnostic reasoning.
